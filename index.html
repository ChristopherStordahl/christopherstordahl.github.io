01 / About

Research, ethics,
and lived experience.

My research interests centre on how lived experience can inform the study of behaviour and cognition.

I graduated with Merit from the MSc Human Cognitive Neuropsychology programme at the University of Edinburgh. I also hold a BA in Psychology, summa cum laude, with a minor in Philosophy from the University of New Mexico.

My work on cerebral visual impairment (CVI) developed from an initial review of research approaches into an MSc systematic review and evidence map. In a separate project at the SPV Culture & Cognition Lab, I helped develop a structured framework for psychological assessment data to support research on cultural validity.

Accessibility, disability advocacy, and responsible research practice are central to my interests. My experience in museum education, technical support, and clinic management also informs how I communicate, support colleagues, and respond to individual needs.

MSc dissertation · University of Edinburgh

Seeing with the BrainMapping Current Methods and Interventions in CVI

For my dissertation, I conducted a systematic review and evidence map of 198 studies on CVI assessment and support. I examined how variation in definitions, measures, and reporting affects comparison and synthesis across the literature.

Approach
Systematic review & evidence mapping
Methods
Covidence, R & VOSviewer
Document

PDF · 817 KB · Download original Word file (740 KB)

Read a plain-language research summary

I brought together studies on how CVI is assessed and supported, mapping the tools, approaches, and gaps in the evidence.

Differences in definitions and reporting made it difficult to compare studies or combine their results. I argued for clearer shared criteria and more consistent reporting of outcomes that matter in everyday life.

I carried out the review as a single reviewer, and only a small subset of results could be pooled. Those limits matter when interpreting the findings.

Cultural validity & forensic assessment research03

SPV Culture & Cognition Lab

May 2023–August 2024

I helped develop a framework for coding psychological assessment data for research on cultural validity, particularly in forensic assessment, and mentored undergraduate research assistants.

Assessment-scoring frameworksResearch confidentialityCultural validityRA mentoring
Explore the SPV lab workClose the SPV lab work

Study context

The study examined cultural validity in assessment and treatment, with particular attention to forensic assessments and justice-involved adults from minority backgrounds.

Assessment and data preparation

I studied a broad range of psychological assessments and translated their scoring requirements into a structured framework suitable for implementation in R. This work prepared the case-file data for subsequent analysis.

I also redacted and digitised competency-to-stand-trial case files and mentored undergraduate research assistants. My responsibility focused on data and framework preparation for subsequent analysis.

Confidentiality and research responsibility

Working with sensitive case files required careful redaction, encryption, and protection of confidentiality. I approached these responsibilities with attention to the risks of exploitation and histories of research mistreatment affecting the communities represented.
